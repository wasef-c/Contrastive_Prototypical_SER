Each arm

What it trains on, and why

rl_hard

done62.40 / 68.63
target = one-hot on the majority vote

The baseline. It exists because these arms use MSP-Podcast's official Train/Development split, which costs 46% of the angry four-class rows and makes every other arm in the config an invalid reference.

role  reference point, nothing more.

rl_raw

done63.41 / 68.67
target = the five annotators' distribution, as recorded

Tests the simplest form of "labels are continuous": keep the panel's spread instead of forcing it to a spike. The strongest arm so far, +1.01 UAR on IEMOCAP and +0.97 accuracy on SAMSEMO.

It also pushes neutral recall up about 4 points on both corpora, paid for by happy and sad. That is the opposite direction from the affect-geometry mixup target, which moved recall out of neutral.

works  but not novel. Label-distribution learning for speech emotion is published.

rl_dir

failed63.03 / 68.53
target = (1 − α) · hard + α · distribution, and loss weight ∝ α

α is the share of a panel's own disagreement that runs along label-pair axes carrying real structure where that utterance sits in VAD space. The idea: trust the panel where its split is meaningful, collapse it where the annotators are simply unreliable about that distinction.

It lost to rl_raw on all six comparisons, two corpora by three metrics. Per-class recall shows the levers firing as designed, moving recall out of neutral and into sad, so the machinery worked and the allocation it produced was wrong.

failed  trusting every panel equally beats trusting them by the map.

rl_dir_hi

failed62.23 / 67.37
as rl_dir, with α × 4 and the weight floor at 0

Ran to test whether rl_dir's null was underpowering. Mean α rises 0.128 to 0.456, with 37% of rows effectively hard and 20% fully soft, so the arm separates the two populations instead of nudging everything.

It came out the worst arm on UAR on both corpora, below even hard labels. Turning the mechanism up made it worse, which is the one result underpowering cannot explain.

decisive  dose-response runs the wrong way. α mis-allocates trust.

rl_lowrank

running—
target = distribution projected onto 3 principal components, reconstructed

The panel's distribution sits near a low-dimensional surface in the label simplex. On a class-balanced probe, audio recovers its first three directions at R² 0.64, 0.50 and 0.30, while the fourth and fifth come back negative. So part of every rl_raw target points somewhere the signal cannot go.

One line different from the arm that works, and motivated by a measurement rather than a hunch.

caveat  it makes targets softer, not cleaner: entropy 0.708 against raw 0.486, and the argmax moves on 11.8% of rows. If it beats rl_raw, softness alone is a live explanation and the projection gets no credit without a matched-entropy control.

rl_kernel

queued—
target = P(another annotator says b | one said a)

Label smoothing whose shape is measured instead of uniform. A unanimous angry utterance gets mass on the classes annotators actually substitute for angry, not spread evenly over everything.

Two properties no other arm here has. It covers every row including unanimous ones, where per-utterance targets reach only 78%. And it is a single 4×4 matrix with nothing to tune, so it transfers to corpora with no annotator panels at all.

The measured kernel has average smoothing strength 0.448, and sad's row is the striking one: given one annotator said sad, another is likelier to say neutral (0.425) than sad (0.385).

best story  the only arm whose mechanism would survive outside this corpus.

rl_pc3

planned—
hard label + auxiliary head regressing the 3-d PC position

Keeps the classifier and adds the continuum as a second task. The justification is direct: audio predicts PC1 at R² 0.641, better than it predicts valence at 0.598. PC1 is the neutral-happy contrast, which carries 36% of annotator variance and is the direction VAD sees worst.

next  the first arm to treat the continuum as a target rather than as a relabelling.

rl_vad3

planned—
hard label + auxiliary head regressing valence, arousal, dominance

Identical to rl_pc3 in every other respect. Without it, any gain from rl_pc3 reads as "adding a 3-d auxiliary regression helps", which is weaker and already known.

control  runs after rl_pc3, not before.

Before reading anything above as an effect

Three caveats

Every arm is one run. On the official split, changing the seed produces bitwise identical logits — max absolute difference 0.0, final train loss matching to five decimals. The default split draws train/validation with the run seed, so seeds there train on different rows; the official split fixes membership, and nothing else in training contributes measurable variance.

That has an odd consequence worth keeping straight. A paired comparison between two arms carries no run-to-run noise, so the differences above are exact. But there is also no way to estimate how much any of them depends on the particular initialisation, which is the first thing a reviewer asks. Setting train_subsample_frac: 0.9 would restore real variance, since the subsample is drawn with the run seed.

Not comparable to the mixup table. The official split drops 46% of the angry four-class rows, 27% of happy and sad. Every number here lives in its own family, which is why rl_hard exists.

The R² figures come from a model trained on hard labels. Those embeddings were optimised to discard within-class variation, which is partly what PC1 represents. That biases the predictability test against the continuum, so 0.641 is encouraging — but predicting something well and that something helping classification are different claims.
