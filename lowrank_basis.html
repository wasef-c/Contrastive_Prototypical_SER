rl_lowrank / MSP-Podcast official Train, 84,260 utterances

The low-rank label basis

The target is built from annotator votes alone. VAD appears nowhere in it — and the largest direction in the vote data is the one VAD recovers least well, though it does recover about a third of it.

Does it use VAD?

No, at any point

The basis is fitted to the nine-way distribution of annotator votes. Valence, arousal and dominance are not inputs to the fit, not inputs to the target, and not inputs to the model in this arm — use_proto_predictor, use_muted_mixup and use_vad_soft_labels are all off.

That is worth sitting with, because it inverts the project's premise. Every arm that used VAD lost: the affect-geometry mixup family, and rl_dir and rl_dir_hi, whose trust weights were conditioned on VAD cells. The arm that works ignores VAD completely and reads only the structure of the votes.

Not using VAD is a fact about the arm. It is not evidence that the basis is independent of VAD, which is a separate question and is answered below — less favourably.

The construction

Four steps, no free parameters except the rank

1

Start from the votes

Five annotators rated MSP-PODCAST_0016_0352: three neutral, two happy. As proportions over the nine labels that is a point in a nine-dimensional simplex.

p = [0.60, 0.40, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0]
2

Find the surface those points lie on

Across 84,260 official-Train utterances the vote vectors do not fill all nine dimensions. Principal components of their covariance give the directions they actually vary along. Three components hold 76.1% of the variance.

3

Project and reconstruct

Keep only the first three directions, then map back to the nine labels. Anything the votes did outside that subspace is discarded.

p' = mean + (p − mean) V₃ V₃ᵀ
4

Renormalise over the four trained classes

The classifier has four outputs, so the reconstruction is restricted to neutral, happy, sad and angry and rescaled to sum to one. That becomes the soft target.

target = [0.589, 0.391, 0.006, 0.014]

The rank is the only choice, and it was set by a separate measurement rather than tuned: on a class-balanced probe, audio recovered the first three directions and not the fourth or fifth.

Against VAD

This page previously claimed PC2 "is" valence and PC1 is independent of VAD. Both were wrong.

Nonlinearly, VAD recovers 59.4% of PC2 — substantial, but 40% remains, so it is not valence in other coordinates. PC3 at 42.2% is weaker still. And PC1 is not independent of VAD: the three dimensions jointly recover 32.4% of it, which low pairwise correlations concealed.

What survives is narrower and still worth something: PC1 is the least VAD-predictable of the three and simultaneously the largest, at 38.6% of the variance. The ordering by predictability is the opposite of the ordering by size.

Robustness

Three checks that change the reading

Rank three is largely forced, not discovered

A four-class probability vector sums to one, so it occupies exactly three independent coordinates. The nine-label fit escapes that by construction — but only if the rare labels carry real mass.

quantityvalue
mean vote mass inside the four classes0.752
rows with ALL mass in the four classes37.8%
variance in the four-class coordinates79.9%
variance in the five rare coordinates20.1%

The four-class block holds 79.9% of the variance and spans exactly three dimensions by construction. "Rank three keeps 76.1%" is therefore close to restating that, not an independent discovery about emotion. The 20.1% outside it is the part that is genuinely about the wider label set.

A third of the residual is annotation noise

The observed distribution is a five-person estimate, so part of its variance is sampling. The multinomial correction used elsewhere in this project applies here too.

component of the varianceshare
sampling noise37.1%
explained by VAD23.4% observed → 37.1% of true
true signal VAD misses39.6%

So the earlier figure of "VAD explains only 25.4%" understated VAD. Against the variance that is actually signal, it explains 37.1%. The residual is not all nuance: 37.1% of what VAD fails to explain is noise no predictor could reach.

The subspace is stable, the third direction less so

Neutral and happy are the two most frequent classes, so PC1 loading on them could be frequency. Compared as subspaces by principal angle, not as individual directions.

The subspace is not a frequency artefact: under balancing the first two directions move 0.1° and 1.0°, against 80.5° for unrelated subspaces. But the third rotates 12.8°, so PC3 specifically does depend on the class mix and any claim resting on it should be hedged.

IEMOCAP UAR is positive on all three seeds, paired sd 0.26, so mean over standard error is 4.2. SAMSEMO UAR is zero and its sign flips, but accuracy and F1 are positive on all three seeds and larger than on IEMOCAP.

Softness is not ruled out as the explanation. Projecting to rank 3 raises mean target entropy from 0.486 to 0.708, and UAR in this setup has risen monotonically with target softness across every arm. Nothing here separates "the three-dimensional structure matters" from "flatter targets help". The control for that is rl_raw flattened by temperature to entropy 0.708 with no projection, and it has not been run.

The projection also moves the argmax on 11.8% of rows, so one row in nine trains toward a different class than its panel's majority. A single full-data run showed +1.30 IEMOCAP UAR; three seeds at 90% data put it at +0.62, so that first figure was a favourable draw.

Loadings, variance shares and VAD correlations are the committed basis the arm trained on. The rank-3 choice rests on an audio-predictability probe fitted separately on a 10,000-row class-balanced sample, whose variance shares differ from this fit — the directions agree, the percentages do not. VAD prediction is cross-validated with speakers held out across 2,112 speakers; the noise decomposition uses the unbiased multinomial estimate (1 − ||p||²)/(n − 1); subspaces are compared by principal angle.
